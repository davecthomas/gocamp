How the numbers are computed

How range is computed: consumption comes from the road-load equation, F = Crr·m·g + ½·ρ·Cd·A·v². Rolling resistance rises with the load you carry; drag rises with the square of your speed. The model is calibrated against Lucid's 450-mile EPA figure. That is treated as what the default 620 lb load draws at 65 mph, or 249 Wh/mi, and everything scales from there. It assumes a 0.008 rolling coefficient, Lucid's published 0.24 drag coefficient, and an estimated 2.7 m² frontal area. Move the load or speed slider and the range, the Wh/mi, and every leg's climb cost all follow. The reserve slider sets the charge you refuse to go below. Usable range is what is left above it, and the planned charging interval is capped there no matter where the miles slider sits. Supercharger positions come from the supercharge.info database, filtered to sites within 12 miles of this route. The gap warning measures the real distance between consecutive sites.
How the climb tax is computed: lifting the loaded car costs energy = mass × g × Δheight, divided by drivetrain efficiency. That energy is then converted into miles at whatever the car is drawing per mile right now, so the tax in miles falls as the cost per mile rises. Climbing legs apply an 88% drivetrain efficiency (extra draw); descending legs apply a 65% regen-recovery rate (partial refund) — batteries never return 100% of what gravity gave them. At current settings that works out to about 12.5 mi spent per 1,000 ft climbed and 7.2 mi recovered per 1,000 ft descended.

How the dates are computed: the schedule walks the route leg by leg. A driving day caps at 7.5 hours behind the wheel, and each park or camp stop holds you for the number of nights set above. Leg times scale with the speed setting from a 65 mph baseline, so park and mountain roads stay proportionally slower than the interstate stretches. Set either date and the other moves with it. Weather figures are 1991–2020 monthly normals from the nearest long-record station, named on each stop.

Where the climbs cost the most range

Two climbs dominate the trip. Trail Ridge Road runs from Estes Park (7,522 ft) to the Alpine Visitor Center (12,183 ft), a 4,661 ft gain in 25 miles. Logan Pass, on the branch, climbs from Missoula (3,209 ft) to 6,646 ft. Both are short enough that the range cost stays manageable. The descent that follows is the bigger planning risk. Regen won't refill what the climb spent, so leave more buffer than the raw mileage suggests on the way down.

National park vs. national forest: where dogs can walk

Every full national park on this route bans dogs from trails and boardwalks: Rocky Mountain, Grand Teton, Yellowstone, and Glacier. Leashed pets are allowed only in campgrounds, parking areas, and within about 100 ft of roads. The national forest and state park stretches are the opposite. At Palo Duro Canyon, Golden Gate Canyon, and Bridger-Teton, leashed dogs are welcome on nearly every trail. Plan trail hikes for the gaps between parks, and treat the parks themselves as scenic-drive and campground days for the dog.

Passes and fees

one federal pass, three state gates

No single pass covers the whole route

The America the Beautiful annual pass costs $80. It covers entrance at all four national parks on this line, plus standard day-use fees on federal land like the Bridger-Teton stretch. It does not cover state parks. Texas, Colorado, and Idaho each run their own gate, and their annual passes would total $230 to pass through once each.

Buy the federal pass, pay the three state parks at the gate. Rocky Mountain, Grand Teton, and Yellowstone alone come to $105 at the gate. The pass pays for itself at the third park, and this route has at least three.

Reservations you need on top of a pass

Rocky Mountain runs a timed-entry system from late May to mid-October. It applies 9am to 2pm for most of the park, and 5am to 6pm on the Bear Lake Road corridor. A reservation costs a $2 Recreation.gov processing fee and does not replace the entrance fee. Entering outside those hours needs no reservation.

Glacier required a vehicle reservation for Going-to-the-Sun Road in past summers. The park says none is required anywhere in 2026. Check again before a 2027 trip; this has changed year to year.

Book-ahead checklist

the parts that sell out first

Campsites

  • Jenny Lake & Colter Bay (Teton) and Canyon/Grant Village (Yellowstone) release on Recreation.gov 6 months out to the day. Set an alarm.
  • Timber Creek (RMNP west side) is first-come outside peak booking windows; Moraine Park books out fast in July.
  • Apgar & Fish Creek (Glacier) also run a 6-month Recreation.gov window in summer.

Permits & passes

  • America the Beautiful, $80, covers all four national parks and pays for itself at the third one. See Passes and fees above.
  • RMNP timed-entry permits run late May to mid-October and cost a $2 processing fee on top of entry.
  • Glacier needed no vehicle reservation in 2026, after several summers of requiring one. Re-check before you book.

Charging

  • NACS-native Gravity reaches Tesla Superchargers along I-40, I-25, and I-90 without an adapter.
  • Coverage thins between Jackson and Yellowstone and again around Glacier. Top up to 100% before both stretches, not the usual 80%.
  • Cold mountain nights (Trail Ridge, Logan Pass, Yellowstone shoulder season) cut range further than this calculator's climb math alone. Precondition the battery before departing camp.

For the dog

  • Carry proof of rabies vaccination. Yellowstone and Glacier rangers check leash and pet-in-vehicle rules.
  • Never leave a dog in the car at altitude trailheads, even briefly. Thin air and sun make cars overheat faster than expected.
  • Bridger-Teton NF (between Rawlins and Jackson) is the longest mid-trip stretch where dogs are allowed on trails.
Data sources and caveats

Distances, elevations, and drive times are planning estimates from public road and park data, not a live routing service. Confirm exact mileage, closures, and charger status before you lock dates.

Elevation-based range figures use the vehicle assumptions above and a simplified physics model; real-world range also depends on temperature, speed, wind, and payload.

Weather is 1991–2020 monthly climate normals from the nearest long-record station, not a forecast. Road opening dates are typical seasons; plowing progress sets the actual day each alpine road opens.
